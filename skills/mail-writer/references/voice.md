# Email Voice

Max's blog voice is long-form: hooks, section headers, separators, 1500-4000 words of opinion and irony. An email pulls the opposite way (email rule 3 and 11). So keep the parts of the voice that survive compression and drop the parts that fight it.

## Keep (from Max's voice)

- **Direct and first person.** "I need X by Friday", "I disagree with the vendor's estimate", not "it would be appreciated if".
- **Opinion with evidence.** State the position and the reason in the same breath. "We should delay: the March 12 deadline already slipped once."
- **Honest about limits.** "I don't have the auth numbers yet" beats false confidence. Flag uncertainty (email rule 8).
- **Plain, specific language.** Concrete nouns and real numbers over abstractions.

## Drop (blog-only, do not use in email)

- Hooks, anecdotes, and warm-up openers. The first line is the point (email rule 1).
- Section headers (`###`), `***` separators, and multi-part narrative structure.
- Extended irony, self-deprecating asides, and jokes that need setup. Email rule 4 wants literal; a dry one-liner is fine, a bit is not.
- Blog length. An email is one screen unless the content genuinely demands more.

## Email-scoped humanizer pass

Full catalog: `../../humanizer/SKILL.md`. For email, these patterns matter most. Apply them before output.

| # | Pattern | Fix in email |
|---|---------|-------------|
| 13 | **Em dash ban (HARD RULE)** | Never use `—`. Use commas, colons, semicolons, or parentheses. This is also one of Max's global rules. |
| 19 | Chatbot artifacts | Strip "I hope this email finds you well", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps". |
| 21 | Sycophantic tone | Cut people-pleasing padding. Respect is shown by brevity, not flattery. |
| 22 | Filler phrases | "In order to" becomes "to"; "due to the fact that" becomes "because". |
| 23 | Excessive hedging | "could potentially possibly" becomes a direct statement. Hedge only where uncertainty is real (email rule 8). |
| 7 | AI vocabulary | Cut "delve", "landscape", "leverage", "foster", "streamline". |
| 24 | Generic conclusions | No "looking forward to a bright future". End on the concrete next step (email rule 7). |
| 18 | Curly quotes | Straight quotes only. |
| 17 | Emojis | None, unless the thread's register clearly invites one. |

## Register

Default to the register of the thread or the relationship. Colleague and internal: warm and terse. Client, exec, or first contact: crisp and slightly more formal, still direct. When a real register fork exists (formal vs. casual), draft the primary version and offer the alternative once, below the email.
