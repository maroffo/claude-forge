# Email Rules

Source: https://blog.dannycastonguay.com/how-to-write-an-email/

Every draft must satisfy these. They optimize for the reader's attention, not the writer's comfort.

## The rules

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence says the ask, decision, risk, or update. No warm-up, no context-first.
2. **Bad news first.** Surface problems early. Do not bury them under context or politeness.
3. **Minimize words.** Every sentence earns its place. Delete filler, repetition, and softening language.
4. **Be literal.** No sarcasm, hints, irony, or coded language that needs interpretation. (This is the one place Max's blog irony does not transfer.)
5. **Use specifics.** Names, numbers, dates, facts. "Vendor X missed the March 12 deadline" beats "there were some delays."
6. **One job per email.** Ask, decide, update, escalate, or confirm. Do not combine unrelated topics in one message.
7. **Clarify next steps.** Say who does what by when. State decision deadlines and what kind of feedback you need.
8. **Separate facts, judgment, recommendation.** Keep them distinct. Flag uncertainty; never present a guess as a fact.
9. **Write for forwarding.** The email still makes sense if someone forwards it with no added context.
10. **Authentic, not AI-generic.** Editing an AI draft is fine, but it must be specific. No "I hope this email finds you well."
11. **Respect attention.** Target one screen. If longer, lead with the point then use short sections.
12. **Clarity over politeness.** Direct beats vague. Clear beats polite but useless.
13. **Clean threads.** Same topic stays on one thread. New topic gets a new email with a new subject line.
14. **Strategic subject lines.** Help the reader triage: "Action needed today", "Decision needed", "Update", "Risk".

## Structure template

```
Subject: [Action|Decision|Update|Risk]: <topic>

<opening sentence: the ask or the punchline>

<2-5 lines of facts: names, numbers, dates>

<recommendation or next step>

<owner and deadline>
```

## When to avoid email

Skip email for prolonged debate, unresolved disagreement, or high emotion. Use a call or chat instead, then send a short email capturing the decision. If the user's request is one of these, say so and offer the call-plus-summary path.
